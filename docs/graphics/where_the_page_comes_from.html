Where does the web page come from?

Your browser is just a screen. The only question is what it’s plugged into.

Google’s computer
A giant building full of servers, in San Jose, California.
You are not looking at your own computer — you’re looking at their computer, far away. The internet is just a very long video-and-audio extension cable reaching all the way to it.

YOU
192.168.8.1
The map
a few feet — right here in the room
A chip in this box
A tiny web server built into the equipment on the table.
The page comes from a chip inside this box, a few feet away. There is no cable to anywhere. Nothing goes to San Jose. It only looks like the internet because your browser looks the same no matter what it’s plugged into.
How to tell which one you’re looking at
a NAME — google.com
A name means far away, out on the internet.
a NUMBER — 192.168.8.1
A number like this means right here, in this room.
The proof: cut the internet. A normal website goes dark — its cable was just unplugged. The built-in page keeps working, because it was never coming from out there.
